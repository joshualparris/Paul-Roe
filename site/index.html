Paul in his element

Among people, places and stories.

Beside the river, around Bourke, teaching, listening, asking questions and turning history back into human stories.

The thread through his life

Paul was a historian who believed in stories.

Paul described himself as an historian with “a foot either side of the Great Dividing Range”: Sydney gave him academic history and the classroom; three decades in Bourke gave him pioneers, radio, country people, community and the conviction that history lives most powerfully in the stories of real people.

His own account says interviewing pioneers for local radio, teaching young adults on a farm community and working across a huge western region in tourism convinced him that people need to know where they have come from. He came to believe that stories could do more than inform: they could intrigue, motivate and touch people.

Cornerstone Community

Pera Bore, 1978

After Paul and Robyn spent time at Regent College in Vancouver in 1976, they returned to Australia. In 1978 Paul, Robyn and the McIntosh families helped establish Cornerstone in the country west of Bourke, with local farming families providing the setting at Pera Bore.

Paul later called Pera Bore a “sacred site” in the memories of hundreds shaped by its mixture of work, study, prayer and community.

Bourke & 2WEB

History in people’s voices

At local radio station 2WEB Paul moved from broadcasting a religious program into oral history. He later recalled collecting more than one hundred interviews and learning that local stories gave him a much deeper way into the community.

The surviving archive is substantial enough to occupy nearly sixty hours of State Library recordings.

Tourism & interpretation

Making place tell its story

By 1996 Paul was documented as Bourke’s Tourist Officer. He developed story-based walks, drives and river experiences, and later described a ten-year journey that contributed to the $8 million Back O’Bourke Exhibition Centre.

Decades later he was still guiding cemetery and exhibition-centre tours and narrating the Jandra’s river history.

A sourced timeline

Landmarks in Paul’s life and work.

Each entry below links to the public source used to establish it. Where public accounts differ in detail, the timeline sticks to facts that can be supported directly.

  1. Sydney · early years

    History, faith and the classroom

    Paul said he was born into a Sydney family of storytellers, studied history under a professor who strongly challenged his Christian worldview, and then taught senior high-school students who were ready to argue back.

    Paul’s own account ↗
  2. 1976

    Paul and Robyn study in Vancouver

    Paul and Robyn Roe went to Regent College in Vancouver for theological study. Paul later described this period as sharpening his awareness that he knew too little about Australia’s own Christian story.

    Sight Magazine ↗
  3. 1978

    Cornerstone begins at Pera Bore

    Cornerstone Community began west of Bourke on farming country at Pera Bore. Paul and Robyn Roe and Laurie and Elvira McIntosh were central to its beginnings, supported by local farming families including the Busters and Boones.

    Cornerstone history ↗ Paul on Pera Bore ↗
  4. c. 1978–1987

    2WEB and Bourke and Beyond

    Paul’s oral-history work at Bourke radio station 2WEB became a major historical collection. The State Library of NSW catalogue preserves 132 reels totalling 59 hours and 37 minutes, including interviews and programs on Bourke people, stations, river life and western history.

    State Library NSW catalogue ↗
  5. 1996

    Bourke Tourist Officer

    An Australian Bush Poets Association report records Tourist Officer Paul Roe explaining the Will Ogilvie monument during the Bourke Mateship Festival. Festival notices also list him as a Bourke contact.

    1996 record ↗
  6. 1990s–2000s

    Story-based tourism and Back O’Bourke

    Paul developed tourism around stories, place and local identity. In a later interview he described the Back O’Bourke Exhibition Centre as the result of a ten-year journey; the completed centre represented an $8 million storytelling project for the district.

    Sight Magazine ↗
  7. 2006 onward

    A national story in view

    A 2006 Christian heritage congress at Parliament House encouraged Paul’s interest in making Australia’s Christian history visible nationally. In the years around this work he completed a doctorate in History at Macquarie University focused on the importance of story for a nation.

    Official biography ↗ Interview ↗
  8. 2013–April 2014

    National Director of Cornerstone

    Cornerstone podcast records from 2013 identify Paul as National Director. At the April 2014 Members Conference he stepped down and Martin Watson succeeded him; Cornerstone’s newsletter said Paul and Robyn remained active in the movement.

    2013 talks ↗ Touchstone 2014 ↗
  9. October 2020

    The Outback Historian launches

    Paul formally launched The Outback Historian at the Back O’Bourke Exhibition Centre with a “Concert Under the Stars” featuring Colin Buchanan.

    Paul’s archive ↗
  10. 23 April 2021

    4 Minutes with The Outback Historian

    Eight short audio stories were released under Paul’s name, covering people and episodes including John Dent Martin, Stanley Drummond, R. B. S. Hammond and Florence Nightingale.

    Apple Podcasts ↗
  11. 2021–2022

    Australia’s Invisible History

    Paul joined his daughter-in-law, broadcaster and author Katrina Roe, for a Hope 103.2 history podcast. Across 31 episodes they recovered stories of Australians whose faith motivated public service and care for others.

    31-episode archive ↗
  12. 2022

    Tell Me Another

    Ark House published Tell Me Another: A Storyteller’s Search for Australia’s Lost Faith (ISBN 9780645411775). Paul launched it through events in Sydney, Wagga and Bourke.

    Publisher ↗ Paul on the launches ↗
  13. 2021–2024

    Still telling Bourke face to face

    Bourke Shire records repeatedly list Paul leading cemetery and Back O’Bourke tours and commentating on the Jandra riverboat. ABC News was still interviewing him about the Jandra and bush ingenuity in July 2024.

    Bourke Shire ↗ ABC, 2024 ↗
  14. September–November 2025

    Bourke and Beyond

    Paul published a collection of twenty-five Western Plains stories. The Western Herald later described him as “Bourke’s renowned historian” while reporting on the book and a November signing at Back O’Bourke.

    Paul’s announcement ↗ Western Herald ↗
  15. 17 July 2026

    Still publishing history

    Paul’s indexed story archive continued into July 2026. One of his last published pieces examined W. G. Spence, the 1890s shearers’ conflict and the burning of the paddle steamer Rodney on the Darling River.

    Story archive ↗
  16. September 2026

    A documentary project still underway

    In the days immediately before Paul’s death, filmmaker Chris Gilbert announced a documentary collaboration with Paul about entrepreneur Thomas Sutcliffe Mort, describing the planned Bourke event as a celebration of almost fifty years of Paul’s work.

    Lamp Post Media announcement ↗
  17. 19 September 2026

    A story entrusted to others

    Paul passed peacefully into glory in the early hours of Saturday morning. His family said he held on “with joy, courage, love and humour.”

    Family announcement · 19 September 2026
